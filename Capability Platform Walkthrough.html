Onboarding any business · part 2

Capabilities, entitlements and the assembled dashboard

One codebase, many kinds of shop. This walks you through proving that — set two tenants up differently, watch their screens diverge, then try to get past the screens with the API and be refused.

The idea in one screen

A tenant is one shape × a set of capabilities. Everything you test below rests on keeping those two separate.

Shape — what kind of counter

Retail, pharmacy, distribution, storefront. Decides wording and which dashboard, and seeds sensible starting capabilities. Never has the last word.

Capability — what this shop actually does

Twelve switches: batches, expiry, FEFO, serial/IMEI, condition, loose selling, prescriptions, field sales, journeys, collections, instalments, dealer pricing.

Why two axes and not one list

A mobile shop and a furniture showroom are the same shape and differ only by capability. Two distributors, one selling on terms and one not, are also the same shape. If shape and capability were one list, every new customer would need a new business type — which is exactly what this design exists to avoid.

Shape and capabilities

1

Find the switches

Log in as owner.mobile@myplus.com.

  1. Sidebar → Settings → Configuration.
  2. Look for two groups: “What kind of business this is” and “What this business does”.
Expect

A Type of business dropdown, and twelve labelled switches below it — “Track stock in batches”, “Track individual serial / IMEI numbers”, “Sell on installments”, and so on. Each has a plain-English explanation written for a shopkeeper, not an engineer.

Worth noticing

These two groups come from a different service than the settings below them — capabilities are owned centrally so every part of the system gets the same answer. You should not be able to tell from the screen, and that is the point.

2

Pick a shape and watch it seed

  1. Set Type of business to Retail counter / POS.
  2. Reload the Configuration screen and read the twelve switches.
Expect

Retail turns on Sell on installments and Dealer and tier pricing, and leaves batches, expiry, FEFO, serial and prescriptions off — a retail counter is not a pharmacy.

This surprises people, so it is worth stating

Retail does not switch serial/IMEI on, because a furniture showroom is also retail. A mobile shop turns it on itself. That is deliberate: the shape is a starting point, never a decision.

3

Prove your own choice beats the profile

This is the rule that makes shapes safe to offer at all.

  1. Switch Track individual serial / IMEI numbers ON.
  2. Now change Type of business to Pharmacy / dispensing — a profile that does not include serial tracking.
  3. Reload and look at the serial switch again.
  4. Set the shape back to Retail counter / POS.
Expect

Serial tracking stays ON throughout. A profile fills in what you have not decided; it never overwrites what you have. If changing a profile could destroy your settings, the only safe advice would be “never change your profile” — which is a trap, not a setting.

What the switches actually do

4

Switch something off and watch it leave

  1. Still as the mobile owner, switch Sell on installments off.
  2. Open Sell in the sidebar.
  3. Open the Dashboard and look at the tiles across the top.
  4. Switch it back on and re-check both.
Expect

With it off: no Installment plans entry in the Sell menu, and no On Terms tile on the dashboard. With it on: both return, and the tile sorts to the front of the row — a capability you deliberately switched on matters more than a generic count.

The part you cannot see, which matters more

With the capability off, the server also stops calculating the tile’s number. You are not paying for a feature you cannot see — the tile and the query are switched off together.

5

⭐ Try to get past the screen

The one test that separates this from cosmetics. Hiding a menu stops nobody who knows the address.

  1. Switch Sell on installments off again.
  2. Go to Sell → Sale and try to record a sale on instalment terms — or, if the panel is hidden, simply confirm it is gone and read the note below.
Expect a refusal, not a missing button

The server refuses with “This is not switched on for your business.” — and refuses before the sale is written, so nothing is half-recorded. It also never names the setting, because a message describing your configuration would tell anyone probing what a tenant has.

Six writes are protected this way

Selling on terms · selling loose from a pack · assigning a territory · settling a driver’s cash · recording or dispensing a prescription · creating a price rule. Each refuses on the server, not just in the menu.

A second shop, so the claims mean something

6

Set up an agri-chem dealer

Log out; log in as owner.pesticide@myplus.com.

  1. Settings → Configuration → set Type of business to Pharmacy / dispensing.
  2. Reload and read the switches.
Expect

Batches, expiry, sell-nearest-expiry-first, loose selling and prescriptions all switch on together — the pharmacy profile.

7

Correct the profile for this business

A pesticide counter needs batch and expiry, but is not prescription-controlled. Same shape, different shop.

  1. Switch Require a prescription for controlled items off.
  2. Check the sidebar for a Pharmacy group.
  3. Confirm batch-related screens are still there.
Expect

Prescription screens go; batch and expiry stay. The dealer keeps exactly the parts of “pharmacy” that describe their trade — which is the two-axis model doing the work a vertical never could.

The two contrasts

8

Two shops, same shape, different tills

  1. Log in as owner.business@myplus.com (plain POS). Set its shape to Retail counter / POS — the same shape the mobile shop uses.
  2. Open Purchase, Sell → Sale, and Register → Product.
Expect

No Serial / IMEI field, no Condition selector, no Tracking row on the product form — while the mobile shop, on the identical shape, has all three. The difference is what each shop does, not what either is called.

Known limits

Stated so nothing surprises you mid-test:

  • A capability change applies to your session immediately, other sessions on their next login. Capabilities travel in the sign-in token, which is what keeps them off every hot path. A colleague already signed in sees the change when they next sign in.
  • Three capabilities are not yet enforced on the server — journey planning (the feature does not exist yet), sell-nearest-expiry-first (a behaviour rather than an action), and batch/expiry (they ride the ordinary purchase path and need that flow reviewed first). They still hide their screens.
  • Shape presets are starting points only. Nothing re-applies a preset behind you; if you want a switch changed, change it.
