Shafiq Medicine Order Run

OMS · distribution pre-sales · end-to-end

One order to Shafiq Medicine, from the shop counter to the cash box

Fourteen cases following a single order all the way through, plus the refusals that protect it. Each one names who does it, what to press, and the figure that proves it worked.

The chain you are testing

book→review→confirm→pack →dispatch = invoice→deliver→settle = cash to AR

Two things in that line are worth knowing before you start, because they are where most of the design argument went. The invoice is raised at DISPATCH, not at booking — so a booked order can still be amended or rejected without editing or voiding an issued invoice. And the money reaches the ledger at SETTLEMENT, not when the delivery is keyed — so the day-end count is a real control rather than a report nobody has to run. The cost, stated rather than hidden: the outlet's statement lags by the hours between delivery and cash-up.

The main line

1

The rep books the order at the counter

order booker
  1. Log in as the field rep, not the owner.
  2. Sidebar → Book order (it is first in that menu — for a rep it is the only entry they use).
  3. Choose Shafiq Medicine. Read the credit banner that appears.
  4. Add both products, quantities 10 and 5. Press Book.
Expect an order number SO-… and status PENDING_APPROVAL. It appears under the rep's own recent orders. No invoice exists yet.
Watch: re-adding a product already on the order replaces the quantity rather than adding to it. "Make it six" is what a shopkeeper says; double-ordering an outlet is the worst mistake this screen could make.
2

The rep cannot confirm their own order

order booker
  1. Still as the rep, open Orders and try to confirm the order you just booked.
Expect no confirm action available, and a 403 if you reach the endpoint directly.
Why it matters: the booker role withholds authority rather than hiding a button. A rep who can approve their own orders is not a review step.
3

The office amends the order before approving

owner / admin
  1. Log in as the owner. Orders → open the pending order.
  2. Change a quantity (10 → 8) and save the amendment.
Expect the order total to fall, and an amendment audit entry naming who changed what.
Why the trail: with two people able to edit — the rep and the office — "who dropped the price?" is otherwise unanswerable. A price change is also re-checked against the shop's margin policy, so an amendment cannot quietly sell below cost.
4

Reject one, with a reason

owner / admin
  1. Book a second order as the rep (case 1 again, any quantity).
  2. As the owner, reject it. The reason is mandatory.
  3. Log back in as the rep and open their recent orders.
Expect status REJECTED and the reason visible to the rep on their own screen.
Why on their screen: a rejection the rep learns about by phone is a rejection that gets re-booked identically tomorrow.
5

Confirm the good order

owner / admin
  1. Confirm the amended order from case 3.
Expect status CONFIRMED. Still no invoice, and the books are untouched.
6

Pack it at the warehouse

owner / packer
  1. Orders → open the confirmed order → Pack.
  2. The pick list shows each line and what is outstanding. Enter or scan the quantities.
  3. Confirm the pack.
Expect a shipment number SHP-…, and the order status to move by itself.
Watch: there is no manual "Dispatched" button anywhere, and that is deliberate. Shipping progress is derived from what was actually packed — a status you can set independently of its shipments is a status that can lie about them.
7

⭐ Dispatch raises the invoice

owner
  1. After the shipment, open the order and find the invoice number.
Expect an INV-… now attached, for what was dispatched. Check the trial balance still balances.
The pivot of the whole design. Invoicing at booking would mean an amendment edits an issued invoice and a rejection voids one. Invoicing at dispatch means the paperwork follows the goods.
8

Partial delivery — the outlet takes less than was sent

owner / admin
  1. Orders → the dispatched order → Delivery.
  2. Key per line what was delivered and what came back — deliver 8 of one line, return 2.
  3. Settlement CREDIT (nothing collected yet). Submit.
Expect a credit note for the shortfall, and the outlet's balance to fall by exactly the returned value. The trial balance must still balance.
Watch the attribution: the entry is recorded as keyed from a signed invoice, and the driver is a note — not the confirming party. Nobody has a device in the field, and the system must not claim a confirmation it did not get.
9

The driver collects cash at the next stop

owner / admin
  1. Run cases 1–8 again for a second delivery to the same outlet.
  2. This time set settlement PAID and enter the amount collected.
Expect the collection recorded against the delivery — and, deliberately, the outlet's balance NOT yet reduced.
This is the case most likely to look like a bug and is not. The cash is with the driver, not with the company. It reaches AR at settlement (case 10). If it cleared here, the day-end count would be a report nobody had to run — which is exactly the control this design exists to create.
10

⭐ Day-end cash-up — the money reaches the ledger

owner / admin
  1. Sidebar → Driver settlement. The open list shows who is holding how much.
  2. Select the driver's collections, count the cash, and settle.
Expect receipts posted to AR, the outlet's balance falling now, and the trial balance balanced. Re-check the customer statement.
The one-line invariant: a collection can be remitted at most once. Try settling the same collection twice — the second attempt must be refused.
11

The round sheet and the challan

owner / cashier
  1. Sidebar → Round sheet — the sheet the salesman takes out and the cashier reconciles.
  2. Open the per-stop challan for Shafiq Medicine. View and download it.
Expect the figures to come from the books, not from the order rows, and the sheet to total to what the driver should be carrying.
Known trap: an outlet with no credit cap reads as null in credit standing, so it is the wrong source for a collection sheet. Give Shafiq Medicine a real limit (the setup does) or this page under-reports.

The refusals — what must NOT be allowed

Each of these has cost a real defect somewhere, and each is quick to check.

12

Packing the wrong thing, when the shop demands scanning

packer
  1. Set order.pack.scanRequired = true.
  2. Pack an order by typing quantities instead of scanning.
Expect the dispatch refused, and the hint on screen to say every item must be scanned.
Typing stays possible and is recorded UNVERIFIED — the system must not claim a verification it did not perform.
13

Booking past the credit limit

order booker
  1. As the rep, book an order for Shafiq Medicine worth more than the remaining limit.
Expect to be stopped at the counter, with the outlet's standing shown — not by the warehouse a day later.
The booker and the sale path use one credit engine. If they used separate copies, the rep would be told one thing and the invoice would enforce another.
14

Cancelling after the goods have gone

owner
  1. Try to cancel the order from case 7, after dispatch.
Expect a refusal — 409 at the API.
Cancelling returns stock. Returning stock that is on a van invents inventory the shop does not have.

Cases 1–11 are the main line; 12–14 are the refusals. If a case fails, note the order number and the screen — the order's event timeline records every transition and is the fastest way to see where it diverged.
