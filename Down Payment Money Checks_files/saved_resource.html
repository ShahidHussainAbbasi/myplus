Down payment and Amount Received — what to check by hand

Selling a Redmi 12 to Maaz with 5,000 down. Six cases with exact figures to compare against. Your suspicion is correct: the two fields are not connected, and two of these cases put the books out.

They are not the same number, and that is the whole problem

Down payment

#instDownPayment · on the plan panel

How much of the price is not being financed. It sets the schedule: financed = price − down payment. It does not, by itself, record that any money changed hands.

Amount Received

#sellRec · on the checkout row

What the customer actually handed over at the counter. It is what posts to Cash and what the invoice records as paid. It knows nothing about the plan.

Nothing links them, and nothing checks them

On a financed sale these are the same 5,000 — the money taken at the counter is the down payment. But the plan reads only its own field and the invoice reads only its own, and no code compares them. Type a down payment and forget the received amount, or the reverse, and the sale completes with the schedule and the invoice describing different debts.

The design's stated invariant is that the instalments must add up to what the invoice says is owed. That holds only when these two figures are equal, and it is currently down to the cashier to remember.

6

A down payment larger than the price

should be refused
  1. Down payment 50,000 on the 45,000 handset.
Refused before anything is written, in words naming the field — "nothing is left to finance" or "the down payment cannot be more than the price". It must not create a plan.

What I would change, once you have confirmed cases 2 and 3

Make the down payment the money, instead of a second opinion about it

On a financed sale the down payment is the amount received. The fix is to stop asking twice: typing a down payment sets the Amount Received, and the sale refuses to complete when the two disagree — the same way it already refuses a down payment larger than the price.

Over-tendering still works, because that is a different question: the customer hands over 6,000 for a 5,000 down payment and 1,000 comes back as change. What must never differ is the amount applied to the sale and the amount the schedule was built from.

Cases 1, 4, 5 and 6 are what the feature is gated on and should pass. Cases 2 and 3 are the gap — please confirm what you see before I change anything, because the fix touches how every financed sale is priced.
