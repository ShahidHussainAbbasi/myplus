Guarantors on Installments
Slice R4 · design v2 · end-to-end review · 4 Sep 2026

Guarantors on an installment plan

Two people stand behind every financed handset, motorcycle or fridge. Recording them should cost a shopkeeper seconds, not eighty keystrokes with a queue waiting — and the record has to end up somewhere the shop can actually use it.

01 The journey, end to end — and where it stops

1 · counterPick customer Already the first field on the sale screen.
2 · counterTerms Down payment, instalments, frequency.
3 · counterTwo guarantors This slice. Name, CNIC, mobile, address — twice.
4 · missingPrint the agreement Nothing to print. The shop still writes it by hand.
5 · laterCollect · recover Where the guarantor is finally needed.
The end-to-end finding

Quotes print a document. Credit and debit notes print. Delivery rounds print. An installment plan prints nothing. Capture two CNICs and the shop still writes the agreement on paper — so the record's whole purpose, the signed contract and the recovery call, never arrives. Step 4 is a small slice of its own and it is what makes step 3 worth doing; it is named in “not in this slice” with a recommendation to do it next.

What each choice buys

  • Recall chips cut the repeat case to one tap. This is the single biggest saving, and it matches how these shops actually work — a small circle of people guarantee most sales.
  • CNIC first, because the card is in the cashier's hand. Entering a complete CNIC that this shop has recorded before brings the whole person back.
  • Formatting and checking as you type — 5-7-1 for CNIC, 4-7 for mobile. The mistype is caught at the field, not thrown back after the sale is submitted.
  • “Guaranteed 4 plans here — all settled” is the one piece of history worth surfacing: it is the shop's own experience of this person, and it is the judgement a shopkeeper is actually making. A defaulted history shows the same way.
  • The button says what is missing rather than going red and silent, and the header chip counts 1 of 2 throughout.

Why recall by CNIC does not become a way to fish for identities

Exact match only, and only within your own shop. A prefix search would let staff type 352 and walk a list of national identifiers. A complete 13-digit match cannot be walked — you already have to be holding the card. It is the same distinction as looking an order up by its full number rather than browsing everyone's.

And it never reaches across tenants: the recall reads this organisation's own guarantors, nothing else.

  • The first two are checked again on the server. A rule that lives in JavaScript is a rule until somebody posts the endpoint directly.
  • The third is only ever advice. CNIC is a Pakistani identifier and this product ships in six languages; a guarantor whose identifier is a different shape still saves. The format helps typing and gates recall — it never gates the sale. Only the name is mandatory.

05 There is no second “customer” dropdown

Corrected after review

An earlier draft put a “From an existing customer” select in each block. The owner caught it: “Select Customer” is already the first field on this same form. Two dropdowns both saying customer, meaning two different people — the one buying and the one standing behind the debt. A cashier who picks the wrong one records a plan guaranteed by its own debtor.

So the word “customer” now appears exactly once on the screen and always means the buyer. Guarantors are recalled by chip or by CNIC, and typed if they are new — which is the common case, because a guarantor is usually a relative who has never bought anything.

07 “Two” is a policy, not a constant

if (guarantors.size() < 2) is if (organizationId == 24) with the number moved. A motorcycle dealer financing ₨300,000 may want two; a phone shop selling a ₨20,000 handset over three instalments may want one. So it is a number in Configuration.

And it ships at zero — the most important number in the slice

Measured today: 43 organisations, 6 of which have chosen a business type. The other 37 fall back to General — show every feature, whose preset includes installments. Three more are on Retail, which carries it explicitly.

Defaulting to 2 would stop 40 of 43 tenants completing an installment sale on the day it deployed — for a rule not one of them asked for. A pharmacy selling a wheelchair on terms would be refused at the counter and told to name two guarantors it has never heard of.

So the shipped default is 0, the panel does not render, and nothing changes for anybody. You turn it on once.

“Not linked yet” is a convenience lagging, never a missing guarantor

That guarantor is fully recorded — name, CNIC, mobile, address. What has not happened is the cross-module contact link, which catches up on the next write. The plan stores its own stamped copy as signed and only links to the shared contact record, so an outage costs a cross-reference, never a guarantor. It is also what the record is for: the shop's evidence must be what the person signed, not what their contact row says after two years of edits.

10 Deliberately not in this slice

  • The printed installment agreement — but do it next. It is the missing end of the journey in §01, and without it the shop keeps its paper file. Small: the plan, the schedule, the guarantors, signatures.
  • Photographing the CNIC card. A real want in these shops and a bigger slice — file storage, retention and consent are their own decisions.
  • Any liability calculation. The ask is a record. What a guarantor owes is a legal question, not a column.
  • Messaging a guarantor. The SMS transport is still blocked on your provider decision, and a guarantor who receives messages is a separate consent question.
  • Backfilling the 211 plans. The platform cannot invent who guaranteed a past sale.
