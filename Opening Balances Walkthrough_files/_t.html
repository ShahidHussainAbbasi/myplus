The one idea worth holding first

A shop does not begin trading on the day it installs software. It arrives with a book: this customer owes 20,000, that supplier is owed 30,000. Those figures have to come across, and they have to land in the ledger — not as sales, because nothing was sold.

Everything on this screen hangs off one setting: the cutover date, the day this business became the system of record. It is an ordinary tenant setting (business.cutoverDate) that the Configuration screen renders itself, so there is deliberately no second copy of it here. Opening balances are dated to the cutover, never to today — post one in September for a shop that cut over in January and it belongs in January.

Once the first balance is posted, the date locks. That is not friction for its own sake: moving the cutover after the fact silently re-dates money that is already in the books.

What it writes to the ledger

3000, never 4000. If an opening balance ever touched Sales, every shop that migrated would open its first month with revenue it never earned — and pay tax on it. Case 2 exists solely to hold that line.

Before you start

  • business-service and the monolith are rebuilt from this branch — the screen is new.
  • Sign in as owner.lifecycle@myplus.com. This is the sacrificial tenant; the run posts real ledger entries and locks a date.
  • Open Settings → Configuration and confirm business.cutoverDate is empty. Case 7 depends on starting with no date.
  • Keep Reports → Trial Balance open in a second tab. It is the instrument for this whole run.

The register

Run in this order — several cases depend on the state the one before leaves behind. The numbers are the spec’s own case IDs, so a failure here names the same case in the Cypress output.

7refusal

No cutover date — posting is refused, and says what to set

Do
With the cutover date empty, open Settings → Opening balances, pick any customer, enter 5,000, post.
Expect
Refused. The message names the setting and the path — not “invalid request”. A migration that fails without saying what to fix is a migration nobody can finish.
1⭐⭐

The trial balance still balances

Do
Set the cutover date in Configuration. Note the trial balance totals. Post 20,000 for a customer. Refresh the trial balance.
Expect
Total debits equal total credits, and both have risen by 20,000. This is the case that catches a one-sided posting, and the one to re-run after any change near this code.
Note
The journal arrives through the outbox a moment after the document. If the totals look unchanged, refresh once more before calling it a failure.
2ledger

Dr 1100 Receivable / Cr 3000 Equity — and nothing in Sales

Do
In the trial balance, read 1100, 3000 and 4000 before and after the posting above.
Expect
1100 debited 20,000, 3000 credited 20,000, 4000 Sales completely unmoved.
3balance

It shows in the customer’s balance

Do
Open that customer in the Customers grid.
Expect
Due 20,000, and a document numbered OB-000001 in their history dated the cutover — not today.
4credit

It counts against the credit limit

Do
Give that customer a credit limit of 10,000 — below what they already owe from the opening balance. Try to sell to them on credit.
Expect
The limit is breached by the opening balance alone. A migrated debt the exposure check cannot see is exactly why a credit limit stops protecting anyone after a migration.
6receipt

A receipt pays it down

Do
Receive 5,000 from that customer.
Expect
The balance falls to 15,000 and the receipt allocates against OB-000001 — an opening balance behaves like any other open document, not a special case the receipts screen ignores.
8lock

The cutover date can no longer be changed

Do
Return to Configuration and try to change business.cutoverDate.
Expect
Refused, and the message says balances have been recorded and must be reversed first. The screen shows the date as Locked.
9reversal

Reversing an untouched balance returns the customer to zero

Do
Post 8,000 for a second, fresh customer, then reverse it. A reason is required.
Expect
That customer is back to zero, and the trial balance is back where it stood before the 8,000. The reason is recorded: a reversal is a ledger entry, not an undo button.
10⭐⭐

Reversing a part-paid balance is refused

Do
Try to reverse OB-000001 — the one that took a 5,000 receipt in case 6.
Expect
Refused, naming the amount already paid and what to use instead. A receipt is already allocated against that document and posted; reversing it in full would leave the receipt pointing at nothing and the books short.
11retry

Posting the same balance twice gives back the first document

Do
Post a balance and — without changing anything — submit the same form again, as an operator would after a timeout on a slow connection.
Expect
The same document number comes back. Not a second one. Two valid opening balances for one customer is a state the system otherwise allows, so a duplicate here looks entirely normal and surfaces months later, when the shop reconciles against its old book — the very thing it did this migration to be able to trust.
12payable

A supplier balance posts to the other side

Do
Post 30,000 owed to a supplier.
Expect
Cr 2000 Payable, Dr 3000 Equity — the mirror of the customer case — and the amount in the supplier’s payable.
13reports

It is not a sale, and the reports must agree

Do
Check the dashboard sales figure, the Sale Detail report, and sales by customer.
Expect
No opening balance anywhere in them. It lives in the receivable, not in revenue.
14authz

A plain user cannot post one

Do
Sign in as a user-tier account on the same tenant.
Expect
No Opening balances entry in the menu, and the endpoint refuses if called directly. This writes to the general ledger and needs no goods, no delivery and no counterparty — it is the easiest place in the product to record a receivable that never existed. Owner or admin only.
15scope

The screen states its own limits

Do
Read the scope note on the screen.
Expect
It says plainly what is not migrated — see below. A shop that loads its customer dues and believes its whole books came across finds out otherwise at its first stock valuation or tax return.

What an opening balance does not bring across

Cash, bank balances, stock valuation, loans, tax and fixed assets are not migrated by this screen — those are still to be set up separately. Aging is summary-level too: these balances carry no invoice-by-invoice history, so a migrated debt appears as a single figure rather than a set of dated invoices.

Leave the tenant clean

This run locks the cutover date. The automated gate clears it afterwards; a manual run does not. When you finish, go to Configuration, set business.cutoverLocked back to false and clear business.cutoverDate. Skip it and the next run on this tenant is refused by a migration nobody remembers starting.
