Wait for registration

After a rebuild each service takes roughly 40 seconds to register with Eureka. Test inside that window and every call returns 503, which looks exactly like a broken feature.

Sign in as the distribution tenant — owner.marketplace@myplus.com / Demo@2025!. Bonus offers are gated to that shape, so a POS tenant will correctly see nothing.

P1 — the scheme master no money moves

  1. Open the register

    Sidebar → Settings → Bonus Offers.

    ExpectThe screen opens with the empty state: "No bonus offers yet."

  2. Create a supplier offer

    The authoring form is not built yet (see the gap below), so create it through the API. Open your browser's DevTools console on the dashboard page so the session cookie travels with it:

    await (await fetch('/bonusScheme', {
  method:'POST', headers:{'Content-Type':'application/json'},
  body: JSON.stringify({
    code:'SUP-OIL-10-1', scope:'VENDOR',
    triggerProductId: 1, paidQuantity: 10, bonusQuantity: 1,
    bonusType:'EXCLUSIVE', qualificationMode:'ONE_TIME',
    priority: 100, status:'ACTIVE'
  })})).json()

    Use a real product id from your catalogue in place of 1.

    Expectsuccess: true — then reopen Bonus Offers and the row is listed: SUP-OIL-10-1 · VENDOR · Buy 10 + 1 · EXCLUSIVE · Always · ACTIVE

  3. Try to save an ambiguous offer

    Repeat the call with bonusType removed, and again with qualificationMode removed.

    Expect — both refused"Bonus type is required and must be INCLUSIVE or EXCLUSIVE." "Qualification mode is required and must be ONE_TIME or REPEATING."

    This is the point of the whole model: 10+1 means a different delivered quantity, invoice, cost and tax depending on which it is, so a scheme that cannot say is refused rather than guessed at.

  4. Check the entitlement arithmetic

    Run each of these in the console and compare against the table:

    await (await fetch('/bonusScheme/preview', {
  method:'POST', headers:{'Content-Type':'application/json'},
  body: JSON.stringify({ qualificationMode:'REPEATING',
                         paidQuantity:5, bonusQuantity:1, quantity:15 })})).json()
    ModeOfferPaidBonus earned
    ONE_TIMEbuy 10 get 1151
    REPEATINGevery 10 get 1151
    REPEATINGevery 5 get 1153

    The third row is the one that matters — a build that ignores the mode returns 1 there.

  5. Confirm an expired offer stays out

    Create one with startsOn:'2020-01-01' and endsOn:'2020-01-31', then fetch /bonusSchemes?activeOnly=true.

    ExpectThe expired scheme is absent from the active list, present in the full list.

  6. Confirm it does not leak between tenants

    Sign out, sign in as owner.business@myplus.com, open Bonus Offers.

    ExpectSUP-OIL-10-1 is not listed. The POS tenant may not see the menu entry at all — that is the capability gate, and it is also correct.

P2 — supplier bonus on goods-in stock moves, money does not

The whole of P2 is one sentence: eleven bottles arrive against an invoice for ten, so stock must rise by eleven and the money must not change.

  1. Note the starting stock

    Open Register → Product and write down the on-hand figure for your test product.

    ExampleCooking Oil 1L — on hand: 50

  2. Receive a bonus delivery

    Purchase → New Purchase. Enter the product, then:

    FieldValue
    QTY10
    Bonus (new box, beside QTY)1
    Purchase rate500.00
    Batch noTEST-BONUS-1

    Save the purchase.

    ExpectBill total unchanged by the bonus: 10 × 500 = 5,000 The Bonus box is only visible for a tenant with the capability.

  3. Check the shelf and the system agree

    Return to Register → Product and read on-hand again.

    Expect50 → 61 (+11, not +10)

    A +10 here is the original defect: the shelf holds 61 while the system says 60, on every bonus delivery, forever.

  4. Check the cost was spread across what arrived

    In the console: await (await fetch('/productStock?productId=ID')).json() and find the TEST-BONUS-1 batch.

    Expectquantity : 11 paidTotal : 5000.00 ← reconciles exactly unit cost : 454.545… ← not the 500 on the invoice line

    A paidTotal of 5500 means the bonus reached stock but not costing, and every margin on this product is then overstated by roughly nine percent.

  5. Check the money did not move

    Open the vendor's account, or the purchase record itself.

    ExpectPayable : 5,000.00 (+ tax exactly as invoiced) Input tax : unchanged — no tax is invented for the free unit Trial balance still balances

  6. Return five, and watch the bonus go back too

    On the purchase row, click Return and return a quantity of 5.

    ExpectRetained paid : 5 — no longer qualifies for "buy 10 get 1" Bonus clawed : 1 On hand : 61 → 55 (−5 paid, −1 bonus)

    Without the clawback the buyer returns the goods that earned the gift and keeps the gift.

  7. Confirm an ordinary purchase is untouched

    Record a purchase of 7 with the Bonus box left empty.

    ExpectOn hand rises by exactly 7. Nothing about a no-bonus purchase has changed.

Sign-off before P3

  • Every P1 step matched, including the every 5 get 1 → 3 row.
  • Stock rose by 11, not 10.
  • The batch reconciles to exactly 5,000.00.
  • Payable and input tax are exactly as invoiced.
  • The partial return took the bonus unit back.
  • A purchase with no bonus behaves as it always did.
Do not start P3 until this is done

P3 makes the sale side deduct bonus units, and it changes COGS and margin from the day it goes live. Any tenant that has used the Bon. column already carries a stock overstatement, and enabling P3 will surface it — users will believe the new feature caused it.

So for each such tenant: run a physical stock count, post the variance as an adjustment reasoned "historical bonus-sale stock variance correction", record a cutover date, and only then switch the capability on. A tenant that has never used bonus quantities needs none of this.
