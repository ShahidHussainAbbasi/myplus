Loose Selling Walkthrough

Twenty-two checks, in the order a shopkeeper would actually do them: record a pack, buy stock, sell five tablets, read the receipt. Every expected value below is exact — if a number differs, that is a defect, not a rounding difference.

Before you start

Log in as the business owner. You need the Products, Purchase, Sale, Reports and Configuration screens. Nothing needs to be reset first — every step creates its own data, so the script is safe to re-run.

Menu labels vary a little by vertical (Business / Pharmacy / Store). Where this script names a screen, use whichever menu item opens it in your build.

A · Record the pack rules

A product has to know that its pack holds ten tablets before anything can be sold by the tablet.

1

Create a product that can be split

Do

  • Products → New Product
  • Name Panadol 500mg
  • Price 120
  • Unit pack
  • Pack size 10
  • Then: piece name tablet, plural tablets
  • Tick may be sold loose
  • Submit

Expect

  • The loose fields appear only after you type a pack size above 1
  • Saves without complaint
  • Reopening the product shows 10 / tablet / tablets, ticked, with the loose row already open
Why this one matters: if the form failed to read the values back, every later edit would post blanks and the screen would quietly drift from what the product actually holds.
2

Clearing the pack size withdraws permission

Do

  • Edit the product, clear Pack size

Expect

  • The loose fields disappear
  • may be sold loose unticks itself

Put the pack size back to 10 and re-tick before moving on.

B · Put stock in

3

Buy 10 packs

Do

  • Purchase → product Panadol 500mg
  • Qty 10
  • Purchase rate 100
  • Sell rate 120
  • Batch number: anything
  • Save

Expect

  • On-hand becomes 10 packs
Keep the sell rate at 120. A purchase restamps the product's selling price from this box. Typing anything else here silently reprices the product, and every expected figure below changes with it.

C · Sell five tablets

This is the whole feature. A customer asks for five tablets; the cashier does no arithmetic.

4

Pick the product

Do

  • Sale screen → choose Panadol 500mg

Expect

  • A toggle appears beside the quantity box reading pack │ tablets
  • It says tablets, not "loose" or "piece" — the shop's own word
5

Type 5, switch to tablets

Do

  • Quantity 5
  • Then any one of: press F7, press Alt+L, or tap tablets

Expect

  • The hint line reads exactly:
5 tablets · 12.00 each · 0.5 of a pack · 60.00
  • The quantity box still shows 5 — no letter appears in it
Check the box carefully. It is read as a number in seven places. If a letter ever lands there it becomes NaN, and the line silently turns into one pack — the customer charged 120.00 for five tablets, with no error shown.
6

Add it to the cart

Do

  • Click Add to Cart

Expect

  • The cart row says 5 tablets — not 0.5, not 5 alone
  • The line amount is 60.00
  • The running total is 60.00 — not 600.00
600.00 here was a real bug, found and fixed during U4. If you ever see it return, the till is multiplying pieces by the pack price.
7

Complete the sale, then check the shelf

Do

  • Take cash 60 and complete
  • Reopen the product's stock

Expect

  • Invoice total 60.00
  • On-hand is now 9.5 packs — half a pack gone, not five

D · The receipt and the report

8

Print or preview the receipt

Do

  • Open the sale and print / preview it
  • Try the A4 invoice too, if your shop uses one

Expect

  • The line reads 5 tablets at 12.00
  • Line total 60.00
Panadol 500mg 5 tablets 12.00 60.00
The multiplication must work on paper. 5 × 12.00 = 60.00. A receipt showing "5 tablets" beside a rate of 120.00 would not reconcile, and a tax inspector checks exactly that.
9

Sale detail report

Do

  • Reports → Sale Detail → find the invoice

Expect

  • Quantity shows 5 tablets
  • Amount 60.00

E · Rounding — the pack of 3

A pack of three at 100.00 does not divide. This is where a shop quietly loses money, so the rounding goes in the shop's favour and is visible on the receipt.

10

Set up an awkward pack

Do

  • New product Brufen 400mg, price 100, pack size 3
  • Piece tablet / tablets, may be sold loose
  • Purchase 10 at rate 80, sell rate 100

Expect

  • Saves; on-hand 10 packs
11

Sell one tablet

Do

  • Quantity 1, switch to tablets

Expect

  • Per tablet 33.34 — rounded up, not 33.33
  • Line total 33.34
Why up: 100 ÷ 3 = 33.33, and three sold singly would return 99.99 for goods priced 100.00 — a loss on every broken pack, invisible, on the fastest-moving lines.
12

Three tablets on ONE line is a whole pack

Do

  • Quantity 3, tablets

Expect

  • Line total 100.00 exactly — the pack price
  • Not 100.02
Three separate single sales come to 100.02, and that is correct — each broke a pack. Three on one line did not.

F · The broken-pack markup

Most trades charge more per piece, because breaking a pack destroys the ability to sell it sealed.

13

Turn it on

Do

  • Configuration → Point of Sale
  • Extra % when a pack is broken → 10
  • Save

Expect

  • Saves; default was 0
14

Five tablets now cost more

Do

  • Panadol, quantity 5, tablets

Expect

  • Per tablet 13.20, not 12.00
  • Line total 66.00
15

But ten tablets is a pack, and a pack is 120

Do

  • Panadol, quantity 10, tablets

Expect

  • Line total 120.00 — the markup is not applied
  • Not 132.00
The case a customer would notice: ten loose tablets must never cost more than the sealed pack sitting beside them on the shelf. Try 25 too — expect 306.00: two packs at 120 plus five tablets at 13.20.
16

Put it back

Do

  • Set the markup back to 0

Expect

  • Tablets return to 12.00
This setting is shop-wide. Leaving it at 10 changes every loose price for everyone, so do not skip this step.

G · What must be refused

17

A product that may not be split

Do

  • Create a plain product — no pack size (e.g. Shampoo 200ml at 450)
  • Pick it on the sale screen, press F7

Expect

  • No toggle at all — absent, not greyed out
  • F7 does nothing
  • The screen is exactly as it was before this feature existed
18

Half a tablet

Do

  • Panadol, quantity 2.5, tablets, Add to Cart

Expect

  • Refused, with a reason
  • Nothing is added to the cart
Rounding it silently would be worse than refusing: either the customer pays for a piece they did not get, or the shop gives one away — and neither is visible afterwards.

H · The scanner

Only if your shop uses the scan box. Type into it by hand if you have no scanner.

19

One suffix, nothing else changed

Type into the scan boxExpect
CODE1 pack — exactly as today
12*CODE12 packs — exactly as today
5L*CODE5 tablets
5l*CODE5 tablets — lower case works too
L*CODErefused — a marker with no count
A scanned manufacturer barcode always means the pack. The barcode is printed on the pack by whoever made it, so it can never mean "one tablet". The 5L* marker has to be typed deliberately.

I · Nothing else changed

These are the checks that protect every shop that never breaks a pack. They are the most important part of this script — a new feature that quietly alters ordinary selling is a far worse outcome than one that does not work.

20

An ordinary sale, start to finish

Do

  • Sell 2 packs of Panadol the way you always have
  • Complete it and print

Expect

  • Cart shows 2
  • Total 240.00
  • Receipt identical to one printed before this feature — no unit word anywhere
  • On-hand falls by exactly 2
21

Editing a loose sale

Do

  • Open the five-tablet invoice from step 7 for editing

Expect

  • The quantity box shows 5 — not 0.5
  • The toggle is on tablets
0.5 here would be dangerous: the cashier reads that box as tablets and the system reads it as packs, so saving would re-submit half a pack of packs.
22

The keyboard chain still runs

Do

  • Ring a line entirely on the keyboard: item → Enter → qty → Enter → rate → Enter
  • Press F7 somewhere in the middle

Expect

  • The chain moves exactly as it always did
  • F2, F3, F8, F9 still do what they did

Known gaps — not defects

Do not report these; they are the next slices

MissingWhat happens todaySlice
Buying in boxesA box of 10 packs must be entered as 10 packs at the per-pack cost. Entering it as 1 box records a tenfold cost error.U5
Returning loose piecesA customer bringing back 3 tablets has no path — returns are in packs.U6
Counting in piecesA stock count shows 9.5 packs, not "9 packs + 5 tablets".U6

If something fails

The most useful thing you can send is the exact number you saw beside the number this script expected, and which step it was. A wrong figure on a receipt and a wrong figure in the cart have completely different causes, so saying which screen showed it narrows the search immediately.
