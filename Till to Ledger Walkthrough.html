Part one

Follow the figures

Five acts, about twenty minutes. Each one places a real order and then checks the number that came out the other end — on the invoice, and again in the ledger.

ACT 00

Stock the shop

~5 min

One product, priced so every later figure is checkable in your head: 10.00 each, taxed at 10%. You buy two of them in every act that follows.

Do this

  1. Sign in at localhost:8080 as the owner above. You land on the Store dashboard.
  2. Open Product from the sidebar and add: name Desk Lamp, selling price 10, tax rate 10, unit pcs. Save.
  3. Add stock for it — 50 is plenty for the whole walkthrough.
  4. Open Settings → Tax Settings. Set mode Exclusive, default rate 0, and tick Enabled. Save.
  5. Create the coupon you'll need in Act 04. There is no screen for coupons yet, so open your browser console (F12) on the dashboard and run:
await (await fetch('/addCoupon', {
  method: 'POST',
  headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
  body: JSON.stringify({ code: 'SAVE10', type: 'PERCENT', value: 10 })
})).json()
  1. Make sure this shop's chart of accounts includes the new delivery account: await fetch('/gl/ensureDefaults', { method: 'POST' })

Keep this to hand

You'll read invoices several times. Paste this helper into the console once — it prints the six figures that matter for any invoice number.

window.inv = async (no) => {
  const r = await (await fetch('/getReceipt?invoiceNo=' + no)).json();
  const o = r.object || {};
  console.table({
    subTotal: o.subTotal, taxTotal: o.taxTotal, tradeDiscount: o.tradeDiscount,
    shippingFee: o.shippingFee, grandTotal: o.grandTotal, paid: o.paidAmount
  });
};

The payload sits on object, not data — that is how this API answers, and reading the wrong key silently returns nothing useful. It cost one red test run to notice.

ACT 01

A shop with tax switched off

the original defect

Most shops never open the Tax Settings screen. The storefront used to price their orders as though they had, using the product's own rate — while the books, correctly, charged no tax at all.

Do this

  1. In Settings → Tax Settings, untick Enabled and save.
  2. Wait 15 seconds. The shop front caches the tax policy for that long, deliberately — it's a hot path, and this is month-end configuration.
  3. Open localhost:8080/store?org=20 in a new tab. Add 2 × Desk Lamp to the basket.
  4. Choose Store pickup, leave the coupon box empty, and read the summary.
  5. Place the order (Cash on Delivery). Note the order number it shows you.
  6. Back in the dashboard, open Store → Orders and find your order. Take its invoice number, then run inv('INV-000xxx') in the console.

Before the fix: the shop front showed a tax line of 2.00 and quoted 22.00, then the invoice came back at 20.00. The shopper was charged one figure and the books recorded another, and both halves were individually correct — the disagreement only existed between them.

ACT 02

The same shop, tax switched on

the control

The other half of the pair. If Act 01 passed only because tax had stopped working everywhere, this act fails — which is what makes Act 01 mean something.

Do this

  1. In Tax Settings, tick Enabled again and save.
  2. Wait 15 seconds.
  3. Reload the shop front, basket 2 × Desk Lamp, Store pickup, place the order.
  4. Read the invoice with inv('INV-000xxx').

Both figures now come from the same tax engine, and the shop's own tax switch is what decides whether it applies. The shop front asks the books for that policy rather than guessing at it.

ACT 03

Charge for delivery

4300 Delivery Income

The delivery fee has always been charged to the shopper. It had never once reached the accounts — the sale contract had nowhere to put it, so delivery income simply did not exist on the P&L.

Do this

  2. On the shop front, basket 2 × Desk Lamp, choose Standard delivery, and fill in an address. The summary should now show a delivery line of 5.00.

ACT 04

Honour a coupon

4200 Sales Discount

A coupon is not a lower price — it's a concession you gave, and a shop needs to know what its promotions cost. So Sales is credited at what the goods list for, and the 2.00 is booked separately.

Do this

  1. Note the current 4000 Sales and 4200 Sales Discount figures on the Trial Balance.
  2. On the shop front, basket 2 × Desk Lamp, choose Store pickup, and enter coupon SAVE10. The summary should confirm Coupon "SAVE10" applied and show a discount line of 2.00.
  3. Place the order, read the invoice, then reopen the Trial Balance.

Before the fix: the invoice ignored the coupon entirely and came back at 22.00 against a quote of 20.00.

And 4200 was empty. Not empty for this shop — empty for every shop, since the account was introduced. The posting rule was right and the caller was right; the value was being dropped in silence in between, by an outbox table that had no column to hold it.

ACT 05

Void it all back

the mirror

A reversal has to undo every leg the sale posted, not just the obvious ones. A void that forgets the concession leaves 4200 holding a discount on an invoice that no longer exists.

Do this

  1. Note the Trial Balance figures for 4000, 4200, 4300, 2100 and 1100.
  2. Find the Act 03 delivery order and the Act 04 coupon order in the sales list and void each one.
  3. Reopen the Trial Balance.

Expect

Every account you noted returns to exactly the figure it held before Act 03 — including 4200 and 4300. The trial balance still reports itself balanced, and the two invoices survive in the list stamped VOID with zeroed totals, because a document you have already handed a customer is cancelled, never deleted.

Why this act is the real test. Getting money into the ledger is the easy half. Anything that can be posted has to be reversible to the penny, or the books drift a little with every cancelled order and nobody notices until the year-end.

What part one proved

Three defects were stacked on top of one another here, and each one hid the next. The shop front ran its own tax engine that never asked the shop whether it charged tax. The sale contract had no room for a coupon or a delivery fee. And underneath both, the queue that carries sales into the ledger was quietly discarding any figure it had no column for.

Every act above checks the same thing twice — once on the invoice, once in the ledger — because the invoice alone will lie to you. Three automated tests passed against correct invoices while the ledger behind them was wrong. An invoice is what the shop shows a customer; the ledger is what the shop actually knows.

If something doesn't match

  • The shop front still shows tax after you switched it off. The 15-second policy cache hasn't expired. Wait and reload — if it persists past a minute, that's a real finding.
  • An account didn't move at all. Check the sale actually produced an invoice number. A sale with no invoice means the order never reached the books, which is a different problem from a wrong figure.
  • 4300 Delivery Income isn't listed. Run fetch('/gl/ensureDefaults', {method:'POST'}) — new accounts are added to a shop's chart the first time they're needed.
  • Trial Balance reports unbalanced. Stop and say so. That is the one result here that should never happen, and it is worth more than the rest of this document.

Known limits, so you don't report them as bugs

  • Tax is calculated before the coupon, so Act 04 taxes the full 20.00 rather than the discounted 18.00. The shop front and the invoice agree on this, so it isn't a disagreement — but whether a whole-order discount ought to reduce the tax base is a live question, and jurisdictions differ.
  • Delivery is never refunded on a partial return. Return one lamp of two and the fee stays on the invoice — the van went out either way. The coupon, by contrast, is pro-rated to the goods kept.
  • A rejected journal reports only "Something went wrong". If a sale posts nothing, the error won't tell you why. That's a known gap.
  • Coupons have no screen yet. Act 00 creates one through the console because that is currently the only way.

Part two

Who approves an order, and who moves it along

Part one followed the money. This follows the order — from the moment it exists to the moment its cash is banked, and who is allowed to push it each step.

The first thing to settle is a common surprise: most orders have nothing to approve. Whether an order waits for a decision depends entirely on how it arrived.

Booked by a rep
PENDING_APPROVAL → NEW → PACKED → SHIPPED → DELIVERED

A rep books through Store → Book order. It stops at the gate and waits for someone with authority to confirm or reject it. Rejected orders go back for revision, never straight to NEW — otherwise "reject" would be a one-click way round the approval it exists to enforce.

Placed in the shop
no gate → NEW → PACKED → SHIPPED → DELIVERED

A shopper has already committed and paid or agreed to pay, so there is no one to approve them to. The order enters at NEW, which in this system is the confirmed state — there is deliberately no separate CONFIRMED, because it would mean exactly what NEW already means.

Read allowedTransitions rather than guessing. The server publishes exactly what an order can do next, and the Orders screen draws its buttons from that list. If a button you expect is missing, the order is not in the state you think it is.

TRACE

Finding out where an order has been

3 ways

In the back office

Open Store → Orders, search the order number, and click the row. The detail view carries a Timeline panel that gains an entry on every status change. SO-000342 currently shows one: Order placed · NEW · 18:26. Everything you do in the next section appends to it.

From the console, when you want the raw shape

await (await fetch('/getOrder?id=621')).json()      // status + allowedTransitions + timeline
await (await fetch('/getDeliveries?id=621')).json() // what the driver reported back
inv('INV-000197')                                    // the books side (helper from Act 00)

As the customer sees it

The shop front has its own tracking page at /storefront/track, keyed by order number and contact — useful for checking what a customer would be told, which is deliberately less than the back office shows.

STAGES

From NEW to settled cash

4 steps

Four steps remain for SO-000342. The authority widens as the money gets closer: anyone in the warehouse can put goods in a box, but only an owner or admin can say cash came back.

StepWhereWho can
1 · Pack Order detail → Pack any signed-in user
2 · Ship Order detail → Ship any signed-in user
3 · Record delivery Order detail → Record delivery ROLE_OWNER ADMIN SUPER
4 · Settle the cash Store → Driver settlement ROLE_OWNER ADMIN SUPER

1 · Pack

Scans each item against the order as it goes in the box. This catches the one error the Ship form cannot: right count, wrong goods. Ship stays available beside it on purpose — a barcode can be missing or damaged, and a packer who cannot proceed will find some other way round. The shipment records which lines were actually scanned, so typing is possible but no longer invisible.

2 · Ship

You do not mark an order shipped. You record a parcel, and the status follows from what went out — SHIPPED and PARTIALLY_SHIPPED are derived states. A status that could be set independently of its shipments is a status that can lie about them.

3 · Record delivery

Appears only once a parcel is DISPATCHED, so it never sits on a settled order. This is where the driver's outcome is keyed — delivered, refused, part-refused — and where COD cash is declared. SO-000342 is COD with 95.00 outstanding, so this is the step that says the money exists.

4 · Settle the cash

The day-end cash-up. It sweeps a driver's collections into AR through the books' own allocator, raising real receipts against real invoices. Until you do this the 95.00 is collected but not settled, and INV-000197 still reads due −95.00.

The whole screen is admin-gated, reads included — the open-collections list is a statement of who is holding how much of the company's money, and the settlements list is the record of who came back short. Neither is ordinary operational data.

Check it landed. After settling, run inv('INV-000197') again. paid should be 95.00 and the due should be gone. That is the same before-and-after discipline as Part one: the order screen saying "delivered" is the artefact, the receivable clearing is the property.

UNDO

Cancelling, returning, refunding

admin only

allowedTransitions for SO-000342 also offers CANCELLED. Forward fulfilment is ungated; anything that reverses money is not.

ActionWhat it actually doesGate
Cancel Voids INV-000197, restores stock, nets Sales and AR back to zero ADMIN
Return Raises a credit note against the issued invoice — never edits it ADMIN
Refund Sends money back to a card; only offered where there is something refundable ADMIN

Two rules worth knowing before you reach for one. A shipped order cannot be cancelled — goods on a van are not back on the shelf, so it goes DELIVERED → RETURNED instead, which puts stock back when it physically arrives. And a partial return is a credit note, not an edit, because the customer is holding a copy of the original invoice.

If you don't see a Cancel button, that is the gate working rather than a missing feature — the Orders grid hides reversal actions from anyone who would only get a 403 from the server.

Worth re-checking here. A cancel is the fastest way to prove Part one's fix end to end: void SO-000342 and every account it touched — including 4300 Delivery Income — must return to exactly where it was. A reversal that forgets a leg leaves the books drifting slightly on every cancelled order, and nobody notices until year-end.
