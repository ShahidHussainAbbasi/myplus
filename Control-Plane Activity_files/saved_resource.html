Control-Plane Activity

Five things the platform can do to a customer — grant or withdraw a capability, change their plan, stop them trading, change their business type — now leave a record saying who did it, on whose tenant, from what to what, and why. This page is for reviewing how that record looks, and for walking it on real screens.

01The panel, as built

It sits at the bottom of a tenant's detail in the operator console, under Capabilities. Reading order follows how an operator actually reasons: what kind of business is this → are they paying and trading → what may they do → and what have we already done to them.

A

The chip is the whole slice. ◆ Platform against ● This business — two colours, two glyphs, two sets of words, so it never rests on colour alone. Before E4 the store had one user slot and one org slot, and every row it held had them belonging together. An operator acting on a customer does not, and without this an owner auditing their own configuration would blame a colleague for a platform decision.

B

Before and after, always both. A record that keeps only the new value cannot show a change: a revocation and a re-revocation read identically. The old value is struck through and muted, the new one carries the weight, so the direction reads before the words do.

C

The reason is quoted, never summarised. Six months on, “why” is the only question anyone asks of this list. It is required by the API, not just by the form, so it cannot be skipped by anything that reaches the endpoint another way.

D

No edit control, no delete control, and the panel says so. The store is append-only. A panel that looks editable teaches operators to expect an undo that does not exist — and they find that out during an incident. The absence is the design.

E

Relative time to scan, exact time on hover. “2 h ago” is what someone skimming wants; 2026-09-04 11:42 is what someone writing an incident note needs.

Worth knowing before you walk it

The panel doubles as the save confirmation. Every control above it reloads the tenant on success, so a change you make appears at the top of Activity a moment later. There is no toast — the evidence the write landed is the record it created.

02Walk it as the operator

Sign in as admin@myplus.com. The steps are ordered because they depend on each other — you cannot see a withdrawal before you have made a grant. Ticks are remembered in this browser only.

1

Sign in as the platform operator.

You land on /platformDashboard, the tenant list — not a business dashboard.

2

Search Audit and open that tenant.

The detail opens with Business type, Plan & status, and Capabilities. Scroll to the bottom.

3

Find the Activity card.

It is the last card, headed “Activity” with a count, and carries the note about records being added and never edited or removed.

4

Hover every row, looking for a pencil, a bin, or a menu.

There is none, anywhere in the panel. If you find one, that is the bug.

5

Change the plan to PRO, reason “Manual walk — upgrade”.

The panel refreshes on its own and the newest row reads Plan changed · FREE → PRO · ◆ Platform · your email · just now, with your reason quoted underneath, word for word.

6

Click each filter chip in turn, then All.

“Plan & status” shows plan and status rows only; “Entitlements” shows grants and withdrawals only. The count next to the heading follows the filter. Nothing reloads from the server — filtering is instant.

7

Hover a relative timestamp.

A tooltip gives the exact stamp, 2026-09-04 11:42.

8

If more than ten rows exist, click Show all.

Every row renders and the button disappears. Changing a filter puts you back to the summary — a new question deserves the top of the list, not page three.

9

Change the business type, with a reason, and confirm the dialog.

A Business type changed row appears, with the old and new type, and a count of the capability overrides the change cleared.

10

Narrow the browser to phone width.

Each row stacks — what happened, then the change, then who and when. The filter chips scroll sideways rather than wrapping. The page itself never scrolls sideways.

03Walk it as the business

The point of the actor chip is that these two look different. This half needs a second sign-in.

11

Still as the operator, withdraw a capability from owner.business@myplus.com, reason “Manual walk — withdraw”.

A Capability revoked row, ACTIVE → SUSPENDED, marked ◆ Platform.

12

Sign in as that owner and, on their Configuration screen, try to switch that same capability back on.

It is refused, with the server's own sentence about the plan — and no new row appears anywhere. A refusal is not a change.

13

Switch a capability they do hold off, then on again.

Two rows are recorded — and back in the operator console they carry ● This business, not ◆ Platform, with the owner's own address beside it.

14

As a plain staff user of that business (user.business@myplus.com), call GET /api/audit.

403. Until this slice, any signed-in user could read the whole organization's trail — every receipt and payment, with amounts.

15

As the owner, ask for somebody else's trail: GET /api/audit?organizationId=49.

200 — and every row is their own organization. The parameter is ignored, not refused, so someone probing learns nothing from the difference.

04What went wrong on the way

Three things broke during the build. Each is worth re-checking after any deployment, because each was invisible from the screen that caused it.

Found broken · fixed

Every record was captured and none was delivered. auth-service was the only application service without the shared trust token, so the audit store answered 403 with no body to all nineteen deliveries, and the operator saw nothing wrong: the plan change succeeded, the row just never arrived.

Re-check after any auth-service deployment: make one control-plane change and confirm it appears in Activity within about thirty seconds. If it does not, delivery is being refused — the change itself is safe, and the record will arrive when it is.

Found broken · fixed

Anyone signed in could read the whole organization's trail — sales, receipts, payments, amounts included — and this slice was about to add “the platform suspended you for non-payment” to the same list. Step 14 is the check.

Found broken · not this slice

A column type mismatch crash-looped business-service through two gate runs and presented as a Cypress login failure, which is what it always looks like. If sign-in fails everywhere at once, read the container ages before reading anything else.

05Not yet verified

Open, honestly. None of this is covered by the automated gate.

  • The business cannot see its own trail. There is no tenant-facing screen — only the API. A customer seeing what the platform did to them is E5's job, and the actor chip was built so that it can be.
  • A record that fails to deliver twenty times is dropped silently. Nothing shows the count and nothing can re-send it. Tolerable for a sale, which is in the books either way; for this trail the record is the accountability. Eight are sitting in that state on the development database right now, and the only reason anyone knows is that a test went red.
  • A change that changes nothing still gets a full row — “status ACTIVE → ACTIVE” when an operator confirms something already true. Keep recording it (who kept trying, and why, is exactly the post-incident question) but it should read muted, not at full weight.
  • Right-to-left has not been walked. The arrow between old and new is drawn by stylesheet so it reverses in Arabic and Urdu, rather than being baked into a translated string — but nobody has opened the panel in either language.
  • A third audit producer still has its own copy of the delivery code. Education's record shape genuinely differs, so folding it in would have meant narrowing a live column — the wrong decision to take inside this slice.
