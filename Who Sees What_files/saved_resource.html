Four of your five expectations already hold

I first told you that everyone in a shop sees everything in it. That was wrong, and running it on your own accounts proved it: jawad and ret.kpr see only what they created, and you see everything. Per-member visibility is already built and working.

The rule is their role, not the dropdown — callerSeesWholeOrg(): an owner, super or admin sees the whole shop; a plain member sees only the rows they created.

What is genuinely wrong

The “Sees” dropdown is not the thing deciding it

The choice is stored on the set and written into the sign-in token, and nothing reads it. Both your staff are set to All records in this shop right now — and both see only their own, because their role says so.

So the problem is not missing scoping. It is two answers to one question, and the dropdown is the one nobody consults. Setting jawad to All records changes nothing.

⚠ Three things that will look like bugs and are not

A permission change takes up to 15 minutes. Permissions travel in the sign-in token, which refreshes on that cycle. Have the person sign out and back in to see a change immediately. This was a deliberate trade — instant revocation costs a database read on every request.

Storekeeper may still be able to sell. The menu follows the permissions, but the enforcement covers a named list of paths, and not every screen is on that list yet. Anything not yet listed is allowed and logged rather than refused — deliberately, so that introducing permissions could not break a screen that worked the day before. If step 4 completes a sale, that is this gap, not a mis-grant.

A member with their own token could bypass the browser. Enforcement currently sits in front of the screens. Closing that means putting the same rule inside each service — the next phase.

What is left to make the dropdown real

Smaller than I first said, because the scoping already exists. What is missing is letting the SET override the ROLE: today callerSeesWholeOrg() asks only “are they an owner or admin?”, and it would also have to ask “does their set say ALL?”.

Two decisions come first, and both are yours rather than technical:

  • Can a plain member be widened to the whole shop? That is what All records promises. It means a cashier could read every sale in the shop, including another cashier’s takings.
  • Can an ADMIN be narrowed to their own? Today an admin always sees the whole shop. If the set is to decide, it decides in both directions — or it is only half a control.

Until it is wired, the honest thing is to remove the dropdown rather than leave a control that reads as a promise. It can return with the behaviour behind it.
